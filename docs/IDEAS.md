# IDEAS (a big, deliberately messy pile; agents: mine it, add to it, ignore what doesn't fit)
Nothing here is binding except where it conflicts with CANON.md (then CANON wins). Prefer ideas that make the *place* more real or the *wrongness* more specific. Anything promoted into a task or doc should be moved out of here. Add new ideas under the right heading; keep them one or two lines.

## The feeling, in words
Ordinary dread. A cheerful man narrating a disaster in verse while the world quietly stops agreeing with him. Sweat, damp, heat, dust, wet feathers, fried eggs, cold coffee, the smell of a TV that has just died. Comedy that curdles. The house is generous and indifferent. The moon watches like an animal. Everything is slightly too close, slightly too clean, slightly too late.

## Open-world feel inside a linear story
* Each act's space is bigger than its beats need. Doors that aren't part of the story open onto real rooms with real stuff (no lore, just physical life).
* Let the player wander for ten minutes in Act 7/8 without a prompt; the mansion is the star. Rewarding exploration = small wrongness discoveries, not collectibles.
* Optional readable objects (a grocery list, a TV Guide, a seed catalogue, a postcard) written in the story's voice, never adding backstory; they exist to make rooms feel lived in and to carry slow text-wrongness.
* The player can look at everything; the interaction prompt only appears when it matters. Long, quiet "idle" looks (nothing to do, just see).
* Pacing: a long walk is a feature. Footsteps, dust in light, a clock somewhere.
* Wayfinding by sound: the kitchen's eggs-and-toast clatter in Act 7, the lantern's chirping in Act 4, the fire's roar in Act 9.

## Per-act expansion ideas
**Act 1 (Wisconsin):** the TV's picture persists as a ghost after it dies (afterimage on the CRT glass). The family on the couch never blinks; Frank's head turns to follow you only when you look away. Snow outside is wrong: it falls upward for a second. The fish tank bubbler keeps running after the tank is gone. The phone cord is far too long. Elsa's hand on the remote. The Sweaty Man leaves wet footprints that are still wet at the end. Carrington family photos where Christopher's face is half-turned in every one. The yam: an odd small ritual object set on the floor beside the solution; they sit there like an altar for the rest of the act. Hitting the TV should *feel* good: the glass, the song cutting off mid-note, a smell of ozone.
**Act 2 (Road):** the road *is* the game: the dash lights like a console, the hills as ramps. A radio drifts between stations and sometimes plays Elsa's voice. Mile markers that count the wrong direction. The inn is a lit window with silhouettes who all face the road. The racoon's eyes in the headlights hold a half-second too long and are *not* a racoon's. The back seat is silent and then not. Rain that doesn't touch the car. Trees only exist inside the headlight cone. Passing the same gas station sign twice. The letter on the passenger seat faintly brightens. The "score" of the road is only felt: a better drive makes the music box in the head louder.
**Act 3 (Dome):** the house is hot and very quiet at 5 AM. Cicadas stop when you step inside. The front door is too tall. The dome's glass is warm. The moon fills the dome and is a little too big; clouds drift across it the wrong way. The eye opens slowly: lids of moon-skin peel, a wet blink, a pupil that dilates when the player looks away. His voice should come from the *room*, not the eye. Let the player walk right under the eye and feel looked at.
**Act 4 (Dark rooms):** darkness you can touch. The player's hands out in front of them. Furniture edges as you bump. The vase shatter echoes too long. Matches/lighter? NO (the book has none): the only light is the switch and the lantern. Bird calls behind walls. The lantern's twelve nests: a different bird in each, each moving a little differently; one bird is always looking at you. Dirt floor: footsteps soft, damp. Mary Sue squirms and *talks* in a small tired voice. The run up the stairs: left, right, left some more, as an actual spatial puzzle the player can't solve, only endure; the corridor repeats with small drift; doors numbered wrong. The closet: infinite clothes parted by hand; moth wings; the silk is wet. The cyclone as an interactive tumble: camera rolls, objects pass, no control needed.
**Act 5 (Birthday):** warm and sick. The kitchen is right in a way that is wrong: the clock says 12:00, the calendar is blank, the fridge has no magnets. The candles flicker in time with the singing. The birds eat cake with their whole faces. Mary Sue's face changes between two blinks of the player's eye (use a fast subtle head-bob or a cutaway to hands/cake to hide the swap). Elsa's face lengthens as you look; the lighting stays the same. When Frank says "You see him now, don't you," his eyes are someone else's; brief, held.
**Act 6 (Lake):** an almost-empty act: huge quiet, stars like chandeliers, the lake like poured glass. The canoe rocks only a little. Clement never turns. The bobber is the only moving thing. Let the player just sit. Edith's silhouette in the truck memory: brake lights smeared red across the whole sky for a beat. "Hello, son." Cut to black, cold.
**Act 7 (Morning):** the brightest act and the creepiest. Sunlight is too clean. Dust motes hang still. The chatter downstairs is slightly too many people. Eggs and toast smell cues. The portraits: eyes, and the crowd of 300,000 in the Beatles painting is individually painted faces that all look at you. At breakfast the family's voice-blips are half a beat late. The Gronfiser verse is delivered as a long on-screen verse while the dining room slowly empties of colour. After "I left. When they both died." the room's sound drops out for a second.
**Act 8 (Inventory):** the notepad is a physical object; the handwriting changes as the act goes on (neat -> cramped -> pressed through the page). The statues' staffs: elephant, baboon, tiger, loon; at higher Unreality the loon (the smallest) is the one that moves. The enfilade: one vista through three doors; at the end of it the room seems farther than the building. The fleas: first a single crawling dot at the edge of vision, then more; whispers of dollar sums.
**Act 9 (Melting):** the longest, most physical act. Stack of verbs; each is its own small ritual. Chopping: the axe's weight, wood smell, the split. The pot: huge, painted elephants; it pulls your neck down. The fall: a long, honest tumble. The fire: the only light, heat shimmer, the forest watching with tiny eyes. The flute: sound begins to dement; the dance is camera-circling around the fire, faster, a little drunk, slightly nauseating (Reduced Motion: steady camera). Final: the bed, Elsa asleep, the window, the moon, the eye.

## Wrongness ideas (steal freely; each needs a stable `wrong_id`)
* Pictures: eyes follow; a frame is empty and then isn't; a painting repeats three rooms later; the same landscape at different times of day.
* Doors: 3 doors where there were 2; a door opens the other way; a door is smaller than before; a known door leads to the wrong room (only when not observed); the knob is on the other side.
* Stairs: one extra step; the landing is longer going down; the banister has a gap.
* Corridors: length varies by 10-30% when you return; windows change count; a corridor you walked has a second corridor parallel to it.
* Rooms: 5% too big; a ceiling one metre higher; the chandelier hangs where none did; furniture a few centimetres closer.
* Light: a lamp the wrong colour; a shadow with no object; light that leans toward you; the moon's light entering from two directions; a flickering that matches speech.
* Text: signs/letters swim; the same line twice; a word replaced with its near-rhyme; the "I" becomes "he".
* People: stand a half-second too still; a smile that holds; a doorway figure that wasn't there; faces *older* between looks (Mary Sue); someone mouths along with your thoughts.
* Sound: a room tone a quarter-tone flat; a chair scrape from the empty room; voices doubled; speech arrives before lips; music box detuned a few cents more each minute.
* Objects: pillow count not seven; table seat count not twenty; birds not twelve; candle count not thirteen; the TV is always on in the corner of the eye.
* Screen: (built) hue drift, lens warp, double vision, fleas. More: peripheral desaturation; a faint heartbeat-synced vignette; occasional one-frame-late motion.

## Sound ideas
Room tone per space (fridge hum, cicadas, wind in oak, a clock). The music box (Moon Waltz) as the "mind" sound: it starts perfectly in tune and degrades. Pump organ under the dome. A single bird call as the unlock cue. Footstep material variety (parquet, dirt, rug, marble, grass, gravel, wet wood). Breathing (the narrator's) that quickens when the fleas rise. Voice-blips: distinct pitch/timbre per character; Mary Sue's rises in pitch; Elsa's flattens; Clement's one line is a low plain tone. Silence as punctuation. Doors with real weight. House settling at 3 AM.

## Candidate shocks and explicit moments (human approves each; keep to a few)
The finger on the lips (touch, breath). The eye opening. Throwing Mary Sue in the closet (the grip; sound of her small voice cut by cloth). The face changes. "Hello, son." "I left. When they both died." The fall down the stairs with the pot. The fleas becoming visible as a crawling mass on the page/hands (body horror, brief). A glimpse of Christopher's own hands at the wrong age. If an explicit moment is wanted: the flea infestation in the narrator's body; a violent chop of the axe in the woods (something is in the log); the dance around the fire with the family's silhouettes in the flames. Always brief, earned, never gratuitous.

## Technical ideas
* Interiors: bake lightmaps; use a few real-time lights for flickers; faux volumetric shafts with fog volumes or textured planes.
* Procedural wear: decals for grime, damp stains, wear at door handles; trim-sheet textures; vertex-colour dirt in corners.
* Rooms from data (WORLD_BIBLE) so layouts can be adjusted and routes tested in headless CI.
* A "route walker" test: nav-mesh agent walking each story route to prove connectivity.
* Wrongness as data: a table of {id, threshold, behaviour, params} so a designer can tune without code; a debug overlay (debug menu only) listing which wrongnesses are active at the current level.
* Unreality-driven material LUT: one global shader uniform; materials sample a tiny 1D "sickness" gradient texture so artists can tune the colour drift per material.
* Instancing for chandelier crystals, books, silverware, feathers.
* A tiny "reality anchor" value per room (0..1) that locally damps/boosts Unreality, so the kitchen replica can be *too* perfect.
* Seeded randomness (`Unreality.wrongs`) so playthroughs are comparable; a debug "replay" that logs which wrongnesses triggered.
* Occlusion: portals for the mansion wings; stream the exterior.
* Fire: GPUParticles with additive billboards, a flicker driving real lights and the post grade; embers as MultiMesh.
* Moon material: displaced craters, a baked normal map, a wet eye shader (iris fibres, limbal ring, veins), a refractive cornea.
* Water (lake): flat plane with a mirror-like reflection and very slow ripple; the moon reflection offset from the moon.
* Cloth/feather physics only where seen up close (Mary Sue's dress, the closet); fake the rest.

## Asset sourcing hints
CC0: Poly Haven (HDRIs, textures, models), ambientCG (PBR), Quaternius, Kenney. Records in `assets/LICENSES.md`. Fonts: choose an old serif for narration and a handwriting font for the notepad/letter; record licenses. Moon textures: public-domain NASA imagery is acceptable if its license is recorded. Human base meshes: MakeHuman CC0 export or equivalent.

## Narration and text
On-screen text: bottom third, a warm off-white, serif; verse as indented lines; speaker name small and quiet. Typewriter 38 cps with blip. Unreality text effects (S-04): swim (sine offset), repeat (line shown twice), replace (near-rhyme swap), slip (I -> he), desync (blip and letters drift apart), erase-and-retype. Every effect has a Reduced Motion fallback (no movement; colour/underline instead).

## Accessibility and comfort
Reduced Motion (done in settings): no sway/wobble/specks/chroma/flicker. Add: FOV slider, head-sway toggle, text size, subtitle backdrop, content-warning screen at launch, "calm mode" that scales shocks down, remappable keys later.

## Things to avoid
HUD, meters, scores, explicit objectives, obvious "go here" arrows, loud random scares, lore the story doesn't have, real people's likenesses, copyrighted songs, resolving the Errante's identity, explaining Clement/Edith/Frank, strobing, over-bright bloom, grey-box looks that read as "placeholder" in a hero shot, flat-colour surfaces.
